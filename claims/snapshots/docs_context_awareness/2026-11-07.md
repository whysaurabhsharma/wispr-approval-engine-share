Context Awareness

                Last updated 5 days ago

Available on:
 Mac, Windows, iOS (limited to the focused text field)
Context Awareness reads your active app and adapts transcription accuracy and formatting automatically, so emails sound like emails and Slack messages sound like Slack messages. It's on by default.
How it works in Flow
Flow reads text near your cursor and sorts your active app into one of four categories: Email, Work messaging, Personal messaging, and Other. In browsers, Flow identifies the website rather than the browser, so Slack in Chrome is Work messaging and Gmail in Chrome is Email. Google products (Gmail, Docs, Gemini, Calendar, Meet, Drive, Chat, AI Studio, and Colab) are recognized separately, and one service reached by different URLs or as a web app counts as one app. System processes and Wispr Flow itself are excluded.
Key behaviors
Improved accuracy:
 Flow uses names and context visible on screen, such as email recipients, to recognize proper nouns and preserve capitalization.
Context-aware formatting:
 Flow matches casing and punctuation to surrounding text, unless your text starts with a proper noun matching your first or last name, your personal or team dictionary, or a name on screen. Trailing "!" and "?" are removed only for mid-sentence continuation or when text is selected, and never when the line already has punctuation. On Mac and Windows this matching happens on your device, and it also runs when Context Awareness is off.
Trailing periods in messaging apps:
 Your writing style decides whether the final period is kept. Formal keeps it everywhere, including Slack, Messages, and other messaging apps; Casual drops it on dictations up to about ten sentences, and Very casual on virtually any length. With no style set, messaging apps drop the period on one- or two-sentence messages. On iPhone, the final period is dropped in messaging apps whatever your style.
Emoji and closing quotes:
 Text ending in an emoji or closing quotation mark continues the same sentence, so your next dictation stays lowercase and a space is added where needed — "We had fun 😀" followed by "and then we left". This includes emoji and images inserted inline in rich-text editors such as Slack, Gmail, Notion, Discord, and Linear. Punctuation emoji (!!️ !?️ ❗ ❕ ❓ ❔), text ending in a period, and a quotation mark that opens a quote (such as 
("
 or 
said:"
) start a new capitalized sentence instead. Casing after an @mention is unaffected, and Chinese and Japanese text gets no extra space.
Browser recognition:
 Chrome, Safari, Arc, Brave, Edge, Firefox, Opera, Vivaldi, Zen Browser, Yandex Browser, Sidekick, and Shift are supported. Flow identifies the site from the browser's address bar and tabs, so switching into a browser is recognized promptly even on very large pages; when a site can't be identified quickly, Flow falls back to the browser name without specific-site recognition. Switching away to another app is always picked up right away.
App-specific handling:
 Missive counts as Email in the browser and in the Mac desktop app, so your chosen Email style and email formatting (greetings, sign-offs, line breaks) apply; the Missive desktop app on Windows is treated as Other. File tagging and special formatting apply to Cursor and Windsurf only.
When cues don't apply:
 Context reading is skipped for any dictation where it cannot complete quickly, so dictation is never slowed down. On Mac, a selection spanning two separate blocks of text in apps such as Arc, Chrome, or Slack counts as no readable context — your dictation still completes, but casing and spacing cues may not apply. On Windows, a text box showing only its placeholder or label (such as the "Type a message" composer in Microsoft Teams) is treated as empty, so your first word is capitalized and no leading space is added; existing text is never deleted.
Privacy protections
What context includes:
 When Context Awareness is on, context can accompany your dictation: app info, textbox contents (before, selected, and after the cursor), on-screen text, variable and file names in coding apps, your user identifier within the app, and the apps in your current session. Flow works from text and does not take screenshots. Captured text, extracted names, and counts for a dictation are cleared when that dictation ends.
Password fields excluded:
 Password field contents are never read or included in context, and your audio is still transcribed as normal. Standard macOS password fields are excluded automatically; custom or web-based password fields may be read like normal text fields.
Other exclusions:
 Flow excludes sensitive and numeric-only fields and browser URL bars, filters out placeholder and hint text, and skips context reading entirely in banking and financial apps and inside Wispr Flow.
iPhone:
 The keyboard sends the text before and after your cursor, any selected text, and the app name with each dictation. There's no Context Awareness switch on iPhone.
Accessibility permissions:
 On Mac, grant accessibility permissions in System Settings for Context Awareness to work. Windows requires none.
Separate from other privacy settings:
 Turning off

 (on by default for Free and Pro, off for Enterprise) does not turn off Context Awareness or 
Dictation Cloud Storage

Examples
Dictating an email in Gmail
What happens:
 Flow reads the recipient's name and applies email-appropriate capitalization and punctuation.
You say:
 "hey alex thanks for sending over the proposal i'll review it by friday"
Flow types:
 Hey Alex, thanks for sending over the proposal. I'll review it by Friday.
Continuing a message in Slack
What happens:
 Flow matches the casing and punctuation of the text already in the message box.
You say:
 "sounds good let's sync tomorrow morning"
Flow types:
 sounds good, let's sync tomorrow morning
FAQs
How do I turn off Context Awareness?
Go to 
Settings
 → 
Data and Privacy
 and toggle off 
Context awareness
. If your organization has disabled it for all users, the toggle is off and locked, with the tooltip "This setting is managed by your organization."
What is still sent when Context Awareness is off?
On Mac and Windows, Flow still sends the app name and website domain with each dictation to set the right tone. Flow still reads the text immediately around your cursor, but uses it only on your device to match capitalization, spacing, and punctuation; that text isn't sent, and no other on-screen text is read. Command Mode and Polish send the text you're editing so it can be changed.
Is Context Awareness HIPAA compliant?
It is compatible with HIPAA workflows: sign the applicable Business Associate Agreement (BAA), which locks 
Improve the model for everyone
 and 
Dictation Cloud Storage
 off, and disable Context Awareness separately if needed. Revoking a personal BAA in 
Settings
 → 
Data and Privacy
 removes that agreement's locks but not other organization restrictions, so review the implications first. Organization BAAs are managed by authorized administrators in the Admin Portal.
Can admins control Context Awareness organization-wide?
On Growth, each person controls their own setting and administrators cannot enforce it off. On Enterprise, admins choose 
Available
, which lets each member control their own toggle but does not force it on, or 
Disable for all users
, which turns it off for everyone on Mac and Windows and locks the setting.
